# TAM, SAM, and SOM Explained: Practical Market Sizing for Founders

When estimating market opportunity, founders often rely on top-down macroeconomic statistics: "The global corporate wellness industry is $50 billion, and if we capture just 1%, we will be a $500 million business." 

Investors and experienced operators dismiss this logic instantly. Top-down percentages ignore unit economics, regional regulations, and distribution realities. To demonstrate credible commercial opportunity, you must model your market using a bottom-up methodology across three distinct tiers: TAM, SAM, and SOM.

---

## 1. Total Addressable Market (TAM)

**Total Addressable Market** represents the maximum annual revenue opportunity available if your product achieved a 100% monopolistic share of every potential customer globally.

- **Bottom-Up Formula:**  
  `TAM = Total Potential Buyer Accounts Globally × Annual Contract Value (ACV)`
- **Operator Purpose:** TAM proves that the fundamental category is large enough to sustain long-term growth or venture scale. A category with a TAM below $100M rarely attracts institutional venture funding, though it can create a profitable bootstrapped business.

---

## 2. Serviceable Addressable Market (SAM)

**Serviceable Addressable Market** is the specific subset of your TAM that you can realistically serve given your current business model, geographical focus, language coverage, and technical architecture.

- **Bottom-Up Formula:**  
  `SAM = Target Accounts within Reachable Segments/Geographies × ACV`
- **Filtering Criteria:**
  - **Geography & Compliance:** Can your software legally operate in the EU or APAC under local data privacy mandates?
  - **Company Size:** Are you selling strictly to mid-market firms (100–1,000 employees) or early-stage startups?
  - **Channel Capabilities:** Does your sales model support direct outbound enterprise calls or self-serve digital checkout?

If your global software serves restaurant chains, but your initial release integrates solely with Toast POS across North America, your SAM includes only North American restaurants running Toast POS.

---

## 3. Serviceable Obtainable Market (SOM)

**Serviceable Obtainable Market** is your near-term commercial target over the next 12 to 36 months. It represents the realistic revenue share you can win based on your marketing budget, sales headcount, and direct competitive alternatives.

- **Formula:**  
  `SOM = SAM × Target Market Share Percentage (typically 1% to 5%)`
- **Operational Reality Check:**  
  Divide your projected SOM by your Annual Contract Value to determine the required paying accounts. If your SOM implies 500 closed deals in Year 2, but your single account executive can only close 4 deals per month, your SOM projection is operationally unachievable.

---

## Top-Down vs. Bottom-Up: Which to Use?

Always construct your deck and operating plan around bottom-up math:

| Methodology | Data Foundation | Credibility Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Top-Down** | Gartner/Statista reports, broad macro percentages | Low (vulnerable to lazy assumptions) |
| **Bottom-Up** | Verified census counts, directory listings, actual pricing tiers | High (defensible and verifiable) |

---

## The Rule of Thumb for Founders

When planning your company roadmap:
- Use **TAM** to verify your macro ceiling.
- Use **SAM** to align your product roadmap and localization resources.
- Use **SOM** to build your financial hiring plan, ad spend budget, and cash runway targets.
